## Academic Background

My academic background combines electrical engineering, embedded systems, and computer engineering, with a particular interest in the development of efficient computing systems. Throughout my studies, my focus has evolved from digital circuit design and emerging computing technologies toward the cross-layer design and optimization of memristive in-memory computing systems.

### Ph.D. in Computer Engineering
**Heidelberg University, Germany | 2025–Present**

I am currently pursuing my Ph.D. at the Institute of Computer Engineering (ZITI) at Heidelberg University, as part of the [EclectX research group](https://eclectx.org/).

My research focuses on **digital memristive in-memory computing**, particularly stateful logic and the design of reliable, efficient computing systems based on emerging memory technologies.

A central objective of my work is to bridge the gap between device-level characteristics and higher-level computing architectures. This includes investigating how the physical properties and limitations of memristive devices influence the available logic operations, their reliability, and the implementation of complex arithmetic and computational functions.

My current research interests include:

- **Memristive Stateful Logic:** Investigating device-compatible logic primitives and developing efficient algorithms using homogeneous and mixed-logic operations.
- **Reliability and Device Variability:** Characterizing non-ideal switching behavior, device variability, and their impact on individual logic operations and larger computational sequences.
- **Cross-Layer Design and Optimization:** Jointly optimizing logic primitives, algorithms, and computing architectures to improve performance, energy efficiency, and reliability.
- **Electronic Design Automation (EDA):** Developing open-source tools and methodologies for circuit simulation, validation, logic synthesis, and automated design-space exploration.
- **Application-Aware Computing:** Evaluating emerging computing architectures and approximate arithmetic in practical applications, including signal processing, image processing, and machine learning.

My long-term goal is to contribute to the development of reliable and accessible design methodologies that enable emerging memory technologies to be used in practical computing systems.

### M.Sc. in Embedded Systems
**TU Wien, Vienna, Austria | 2023–2025**

I received my M.Sc. degree in Embedded Systems from TU Wien in 2025. During my master's studies, I expanded my knowledge of computer architectures, digital hardware design, embedded computing, and hardware/software co-design.

My research increasingly focused on combining **approximate computing with memristive in-memory computing**, exploring how controlled computational inaccuracies can reduce latency and energy consumption while maintaining acceptable application-level quality.

Under the supervision of Prof. Nima TaheriNejad, I completed my master's thesis, *[Heterogeneous Approximate Adders for Memristive Processing in Array](https://doi.org/10.34726/hss.2025.130182)*.

The thesis investigated the design and optimization of approximate arithmetic circuits for memristive stateful logic. Unlike conventional approaches that employ identical approximate arithmetic units, my work explored heterogeneous combinations of approximate full adders to identify more efficient implementations.

A particular emphasis was placed on **multi-objective design-space exploration**, using heuristic optimization techniques, including genetic algorithms, to identify favorable trade-offs between computational accuracy, latency, and energy consumption.

This research also involved circuit-level simulation and application-level evaluation, primarily in image processing. Parts of this work resulted in publications in *IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I* and *IEEE Transactions on Computer-Aided Design of Integrated Circuits and Systems*.

In parallel, I became increasingly interested in **open-source EDA and automated circuit validation**, contributing to the development of [ATOMIC](https://github.com/fabianseiler/ATOMIC), a framework for automating the simulation and evaluation of memristive stateful logic circuits.

### B.Sc. in Electrical Engineering and Information Technology
**TU Wien, Vienna, Austria | 2020–2023**

I received my B.Sc. degree in Electrical Engineering and Information Technology from TU Wien in 2023.

My undergraduate studies provided a foundation in electrical engineering, electronics, digital systems, computer architecture, and signal processing. During this period, I developed a particular interest in digital hardware design and the interaction between physical circuit behavior and computational efficiency.

This interest led me toward **emerging computing technologies**, particularly memristive devices and their potential for performing computations directly within memory.

My bachelor's thesis investigated **approximate memristive in-memory computing**, combining approximate arithmetic with memristive stateful logic to improve the efficiency of digital computations.

This early research resulted in my first scientific publication, *[An IMPLY-based Semi-Serial Approximate In-Memristor Adder](https://doi.org/10.1109/NorCAS58970.2023.10305490)*, presented at the IEEE Nordic Circuits and Systems Conference (NorCAS) in 2023.

This work established the foundation for my subsequent research on approximate computing, efficient arithmetic architectures, and memristive in-memory computing.

## Broader Research Interests

Beyond my primary research on memristive computing, I am interested in digital hardware design, computer architectures, hardware accelerators, and the integration of emerging technologies into practical computing systems.

I am particularly interested in approaches that combine **circuit-level understanding, algorithmic optimization, and system-level evaluation**, with an emphasis on reproducible research and open-source tools.

My broader interests also include FPGA-based development, RISC-V architectures, machine learning, and efficient edge computing.
